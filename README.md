One cake, two people who want different parts of it; split it down the middle and both feel cheated. &#9670; INTERACTIVE: <b>drag the cut</b> until each of them, by their OWN valuation, believes they got at least half. &#9670; LIT: each party has a valuation density (Ann peaks at the frosting, Ben at the fruit), normalised to 1; for a cut at x, Ann's share is her density's integral on [0,x], Ben's on [x,1]; an <b>envy-free</b> cut gives each ≥ ½ by their own measure &mdash; it exists and is generally NOT the midpoint (the instrument finds it). Real cake-cutting / fair-division theory (Steinhaus). AMBER: two parties, a 1-D cake, Gaussian tastes; real fair division handles many parties, indivisible goods, strategic mis-reporting. The core &mdash; a split fair by each party's own measure exists and is rarely the equal one &mdash; is exact. LILLITH INTERACTIVE template. David Lee Wise / ROOT0, with AVAN.
